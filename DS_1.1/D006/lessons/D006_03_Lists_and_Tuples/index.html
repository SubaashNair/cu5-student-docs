D06 · Lists and tuples

Unit: DS 1.1 Python Fundamentals · Day 6 of 14

What to do today

Read this brief, then download and run the lesson notebook from Downloads.

Concept taught

Ordered collections. Lists that can change (append, extend, insert, remove, pop, sort), slicing reused from strings, looping over a list, enumerate for the index and the item together, tuples as the immutable form, lambda as a throwaway function for a sort key, and the list comprehension as a whole-collection transform in one expression.

By the end of this lesson you can
  1. Build, extend and reorder a list, and predict its contents after each method call.
  2. Slice a list and explain why the result is a new list.
  3. Loop with enumerate to use both the position and the value.
  4. Choose a tuple over a list and state what the choice guarantees.
  5. Rewrite a for-loop that builds a list as a comprehension, and sort with a lambda key.
Why this matters

A DataFrame column behaves like a list; a NumPy shape is a tuple; groupby keys come back as tuples. The comprehension is the bridge to vectorised thinking, and the lambda returns inside sort, apply and key arguments across the whole credential.
